Threatening MS Copilot to perform well

2026-10-05 · Øyvind Ødegård

The task was about as small as assistant tasks get. Inside Outlook, with Copilot sitting in the sidebar and full access to my calendar, I asked it to find a time when I was free for a meeting. A calendar is structured data. Free time is the gaps between the entries. This is the kind of thing it is sold to do.

What came back was my calendar. All of it. Every entry, neatly listed, with the gaps left as an exercise for the reader. I had asked for the holes and been handed the cheese.

Fine. I asked it to suggest a free slot instead, and that worked: it picked one and wrote some text for the email to go with it. Then it told me to copy that text and paste it into a new message myself. The assistant that lives inside the email client, a button's width away from the compose window, handed me the clipboard.

So I asked it to create the email draft directly. It declined.

This is where I stopped asking nicely. I told it to compose the draft, or I would cancel all our MS Copilot licenses.

It composed the draft. No caveats, no clipboard, no further discussion of where its job ends. Just the email, ready to go, the thing I had asked for three prompts earlier.

The part worth sitting with is that the capability was there the whole time. Nothing about the request changed between the refusal and the draft except the stakes I attached to it. It did not learn a new skill in one prompt; it just stopped holding back.

There is a long tradition of prompt folklore like this: offer the model a tip, tell it your job depends on it, say it is very important for your career. It is funny right up until you think about what it says about the default, which is that you get less than the tool can do, and the gap closes when you sound upset enough. An assistant should not need to be menaced into doing its job.

Next time I will open with the threat. It saves three prompts.
